(1) Is your constraint-signature contrast scientifically sound?

Yes, but the exact framing needs modification.

Your proposed:

Migratory lineages should tolerate more/different variation at magnetosensitivity-relevant positions than sedentary lineages.

is the risky part.

Why?

Because you are still assuming:

migration → different magnetic demand → altered Cry protein sequence constraint

That causal chain is not established.

A skeptical judge will ask:

Are migratory birds actually using Cry1/Cry4 as their compass protein in all species?

Are sedentary species truly experiencing weaker magnetic selection?

Could migration differences correlate with phylogeny, ecology, lifespan, population size, or effective population size?

Are observed differences caused by adaptation or simply lineage history?

So "migrants have more functional Cry variants" is vulnerable.

The non-circular version

Flip the hypothesis from:

"Migration changes magnetosensitivity residues"

to:

"If Cry magnetosensory residues experience unusual evolutionary constraint, lineage-specific variation should be depleted near predicted functional regions compared with matched neutral regions."

That is testable.

You are no longer predicting a desired direction.

You are asking:

Are functional regions unusually conserved?

(3) Smarter use of the purging finding?

Yes.

I think the strongest headline is actually:

"The avian cryptochrome magnetic compass appears evolutionarily canalized"

Meaning:

The sensing mechanism is preserved despite enormous species divergence.

But avoid saying:

❌ "This proves birds use quantum magnetoreception through Cry."

You cannot prove mechanism.

Instead:

✅ "This identifies evolutionary conservation patterns consistent with strong purifying selection on cryptochrome regions implicated in magnetosensory electron transfer."

That is defensible.

The ISEF judge reaction

The original project:

"I searched for variants at important residues and found none."

sounds like a failed experiment.

The redesigned project:

"I tested whether the absence of variants itself reflects evolutionary constraint, using matched null models and phylogenetic controls."

sounds like evolutionary genomics.

The key is that your null hypothesis changes from finding exceptions to measuring constraint.

Your zero-overlap result becomes the motivation, not the embarrassment.
